Net-wealth comparison (buyer vs renter)

Method: both paths are compared as net wealth over 30 years. The buyer builds equity (home value minus loan balance minus all ownership costs). The renter invests the down payment plus any monthly savings at the investment return. Break-even = the first year buying stays ahead for good. Assumptions drive the answer — test a range.

Rent vs Buy Calculator — FAQs

What is the price-to-rent ratio rule of thumb?

Divide the home price by annual rent. Under 15 generally favours buying, 15–20 is the grey zone where the details decide, and over 20 favours renting. It is a quick screen — this calculator does the full math behind it.

Why does the renter 'invest' in this comparison?

Because the down payment and any monthly savings from renting are real money. A fair comparison invests them (e.g. in savings certificates or funds) instead of pretending renting throws money away — that is the opportunity cost of buying.

How long should I plan to stay for buying to win?

Buying has high upfront costs (closing, transfer taxes) so it usually needs 5–12 years to break even. The calculator finds your exact break-even year — if you will move sooner, renting usually wins.
